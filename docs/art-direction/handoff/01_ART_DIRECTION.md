# 01 — Core Art Direction

> Read the [current handoff](HANDOVER.md) and [final subject folders](../README.md). Final subject briefs govern their assets; generated annotations never override gameplay dimensions.

## Visual thesis

SLUR is a TRON-influenced, minimal science-fiction racer built around one strong contrast:

> **A cold, desaturated universe containing warm, saturated gameplay energy.**

The environment should feel vast, silent, severe, and ancient. The active game layer should feel fast, hot, precise, and alive.

## Core palette

### Primary gameplay / energy color

**Marigold / gainda** is the visual signature.

Use it for:

- track boundary energy
- important functional track seams
- pickup energy
- projectiles / weapon effects
- engines and boost
- selective monolith seams
- selective asteroid energy veins
- finish-line energy treatment
- destructible-block internal energy

Do **not** flood the whole world with marigold. It is valuable because it is controlled.

### Complementary environment palette

Use desaturated cold colors:

- blue-grey
- graphite
- charcoal
- cold metallic grey
- deep-space blue/black

Backgrounds stay low-saturation so the playable layer carries the strongest color signal.

## Visual hierarchy

1. Player ship / immediate threats / pickups
2. Track boundaries, gaps, obstacles, finish
3. Near environmental framing
4. Far environmental scale / atmosphere

Background detail may be rich, but background contrast and saturation must remain controlled.

## Minimalism rule

Prefer:

- large forms
- clean silhouettes
- broad planes
- restrained seams
- limited material families
- sparse emissive accents

Avoid:

- dense greebling
- random technical decals
- noisy normal maps at gameplay distance
- many tiny emissive marks
- ornamental sci-fi detail with no readability purpose

## A → B → C intensity language

The same world language scales through three states:

### A — Subtle

- open composition
- fewer fillers
- smaller / farther asteroids
- sparse monoliths
- restrained reflections and glow
- quiet atmosphere

### B — Balanced

- default SLUR look
- moderate environmental density
- mixed scales
- stronger framing
- clear but controlled warm reflections

### C — Intense

- denser environment
- larger / closer framing elements
- stronger atmospheric pressure
- more selective energy accents
- more dramatic lighting and reflections

**C does not mean more saturation everywhere.** Increase density, scale, proximity, lighting pressure, particles, and selective warm energy while preserving gameplay contrast.

A→B→C can be used both within a level and across difficulty/sector intensity.
