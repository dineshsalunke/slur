## Overall assessment

The shared identity is strong: dark manufactured surfaces, cold cosmic depth, warm energy, monumental surroundings and a clear forward vanishing point. A new style is unnecessary. The set needs clearer limits on texture, lighting hierarchy and visual meaning, followed by selective corrections.

This is a still-image art audit. Apparent brightness cannot establish material values, lighting settings or exposure; perspective cannot establish world dimensions. Motion readability, exact geometry and performance remain untested. Studio presentation, closeups and gameplay views are allowed to look different. Differences become conflicts when they change material identity, asset identity or gameplay meaning.

## Decisions — mood and lighting

**Accepted by the owner:** retain the dramatic boost/action golden reference and add a quieter normal-cruise companion to judge everyday readability. This approves the two-reference approach, not new pixels or the lighting proposals below.

**Proposed mood:** cold, immense, imposing surroundings; warm, concentrated racing energy. Cruise retains tension, depth and material richness. Boost adds temporary energy and speed cues without changing the world's palette or lifting the whole scene's exposure.

**Proposed lighting hierarchy:**

1. The immediate driving decision must read first: obstacle silhouette, missing floor, nearby racers and relevant pickups. Priority means local contrast and separation, not making every hazard luminous.
2. Continuous rails define the track clearly, but their halos and reflections should not erase nearby silhouettes or overpower gap edges. Interior seam lights remain subordinate.
3. Monolith seams and occasional environmental fissures establish scale and atmosphere. Preserve their visible glow while limiting competition near the forward racing corridor.
4. Cold, desaturated light separates distant rock/planet forms from space. Foreground engineered surfaces keep deep graphite bodies, with restrained cold highlights where useful and local warm spill/reflection from energy sources.

**Proposed treatment:** narrow readable emitters, localized soft halos, broad soft deck reflections, dark intact surface areas between highlights. Near-white hot cores may be small; large white/orange washes should not replace coloured sources or material detail. Avoid making the whole floor evenly orange or bright blue.

**Proposed intensity rule:** Calm → Balanced → Intense changes density, proximity and enclosure first; selective scenery energy can increase, but exposure and material identity remain stable. Protect the forward corridor and HUD legibility even in Intense. This does not require removing environmental glow.

**Proposed comparison setup:** compose the cruise companion with the same camera, environment arrangement and baseline lighting as the action reference; vary the action/effects state so the comparison isolates boost rather than also changing scene intensity. Exact environment family remains to be resolved separately.
