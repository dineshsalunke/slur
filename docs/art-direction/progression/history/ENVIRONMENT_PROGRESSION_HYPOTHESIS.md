## Evidence and separation of references

The older three-panel proposal supplied by the user in this conversation has a more prominent progression than board 18. Use it to study composition; use `golden-reference/17_golden_reference_FINAL.png` for approved materials, palette and HUD. Board 15 remains unapproved.

The observations below describe visible differences in the supplied proposal. Their effectiveness under the approved darker appearance remains a hypothesis to test, not an established production specification.

## What makes the progression prominent

| Visible change in the older proposal | Why it may strengthen the progression | Draft implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm has broad continuous sky; balanced interrupts it; intense breaks it into small openings. | The amount and continuity of empty space change at a glance. | Design the negative-space silhouette before adding surface detail. |
| Small distant rocks give way to substantial midground bodies, then enormous foreground masses cropped by the frame. | Scale and proximity change together, making the environment feel closer. | Change the size distribution and depth placement, not just object count. |
| Intense fills the overhead region as well as both sides. | The player appears surrounded in three dimensions rather than passing between taller roadside props. | Compose above the track while retaining a clear forward gameplay corridor. |
| Sparse separated objects become overlapping layers of large bodies and fine debris. | Overlap creates depth and makes the dense state feel volumetric. | Include near, middle and far layers with different silhouettes and sizes. |
| Planets and distant vistas are exposed in calm, partially obscured in balanced and strongly obscured in intense. | A recurring backdrop gives the viewer a reference against which enclosure can be judged. | Use changing occlusion of shared landmarks as a comparison cue. |
| The intense foreground is asymmetric and strongly cropped. | Large masses feel as though they continue beyond the image; framing becomes less placid. | Avoid merely increasing the height of two orderly rows of monoliths. |
| Track, ship and vanishing point remain broadly consistent across panels. | A stable foreground makes the surrounding changes easier to compare. | Hold camera, gameplay layout, ship state and HUD steady. |
| Each panel has enough vertical space to show track, horizon and overhead scenery together. | The composition can express looming height and enclosure. | Avoid board 18's shallow horizontal strips for the next comparison. |

## Confounding changes we should not copy automatically

The older proposal also increases warm fissures, bright light sources and atmospheric brightness toward intense. These may contribute to its perceived escalation, so we cannot attribute the entire result to composition yet. First test the spatial changes under a stable colour grade and exposure.

Keep deep dark material values, faint desaturated slate-blue surroundings, localized marigold energy and the compact borderless HUD from final reference 17. Do not copy the older image's bright grey scenery, mirrorlike deck or widespread orange fissures. Boost is independent of progression.

## Next-draft brief

- **Calm:** genuinely sparse; distant isolated landmarks; broad connected sky; exposed celestial backdrop; very little foreground encroachment.
- **Balanced:** clear intermediate state; substantial midground forms; partial backdrop occlusion; several depth layers; alternating open and framed areas.
- **Intense:** huge cropped peripheral and overhead masses; strongly overlapping depth layers; fragmented sky; compressed vista; clear track corridor.

Build one matched-camera three-panel comparison with enough panel height to evaluate overhead composition. Do not use Board 18 as the compositional starting point: its calm state begins too crowded and its escalation relies too heavily on roadside framing.

## How we will judge the drafts

1. With labels ignored, do the three stages read immediately and in the intended order?
2. Does progression remain evident at thumbnail size, where fine debris and texture are hard to see?
3. Are empty-space continuity, apparent scale, proximity and overlap visibly different at each step?
4. Does balanced feel like a deliberate middle state, rather than calm with extra scatter?
5. Does intense surround the player without visually burying the playable track?
6. Does the comparison retain final reference 17's materials, faint cold undertone and warm hierarchy?
7. Can we explain the stronger progression without relying on brighter exposure, stronger saturation or boost?

After review, record which changes worked, which failed and any unexpected dependencies. Only then promote the demonstrated principles into the canonical art-direction documentation. A still image cannot establish readability in motion or gameplay difficulty.
