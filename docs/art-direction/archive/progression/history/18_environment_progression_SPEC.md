## Three steps

| Step | Spatial treatment | Emotional intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm | Widely spaced distant landmarks, sparse debris, broad negative space, long unobstructed vistas | Room to breathe and anticipate |
| Balanced | Layered near/mid/far scenery, moderate landmark proximity, alternating framed and open intervals | Measured tension and forward momentum |
| Intense | Closer monumental silhouettes, denser peripheral layers, stronger occlusion and narrower vistas beyond the track | Peak tension through scale and enclosure |

Calm replaces the earlier “Subtle” label for this three-step proposal. These are environment states, not new difficulty settings.

## Fixed across the comparison

- Deep near-black stone and coated objects; dark satin graphite deck. Do not raise base material values for the intense state.
- Faint desaturated slate-blue depth, nebula and selective cold rims. Neither saturated blue nor entirely neutral grey.
- Localized golden marigold energy. Gameplay lighting remains dominant; monolith seams retain small local halos.
- Continuous luminous track boundaries and sparse irregular interior inserts, never an all-emissive tile grid.
- Matched camera, track footprint, gameplay-object placement and compact borderless HUD.
- Same cruise state throughout. Boost and temporary motion effects are independent of environmental intensity.

## Level and game progression

Within a level, use the three states as pacing beats: establish space, build enclosure, reach a peak, then allow recovery. A proposed sequence is Calm → Balanced → Intense → Calm; it is an art-direction example, not a prescribed level script.

Across the game, later levels can introduce stronger monumental scale, more layered compositions and longer intense passages. Retain calm passages so that later peaks remain perceptible. Do not turn advancement into a permanent rise in exposure, saturation, bloom or clutter. Sector identity and environmental intensity are separate dimensions: this board compares one shared environment vocabulary, not three new biomes.

## Review gate

- Can the three states be distinguished through spatial composition without their labels?
- Do materials retain the final reference's dark values and distinct finishes?
- Is the faint cold undertone present without saturated blue drift?
- Does the playable corridor remain visually clear at the peak?
- Are HUD and gameplay lights subordinate/primary in the same hierarchy as the reference?
- Are camera and gameplay placements sufficiently matched to isolate the environmental change?

The generated board is a visual proposal, not measured geometry or proof of readability in motion. Exact tile counts, object dimensions, rear-view rendering and small HUD text require separate validation. Approval of the golden reference does not approve this board.
