SLUR — Art Material Sheet (authoritative for surfaces)
Purpose: name every element in the world, assign it a material family, and define each family
tightly enough that two people building different assets produce surfaces that belong to the same
world. This sheet overrides no scale number and no silhouette — dimensions belong to
docs/ART_SCALE_REFERENCE.md, identity and mood belong to the boards and written direction in
docs/art-direction/.
Status: extends the frozen art package rather than reinterpreting it. Section 6 lists, verbatim,
the three places where it deliberately overrides package wording, and why. Everything else here fills
a gap the package left open — it defines material names (handoff/06_IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES_THREEJS.md
lists five, handoff/07_ASSET_CHECKLIST.md adds three) but never defines what any of them is.
Parameter values below are first-pass art targets, not measurements. Nothing in the package
supplies roughness, metalness or base colour, so these are proposals to be tuned at a human gate and
then frozen here. Colour anchors are the package's own (handoff/HANDOVER.md §4); the ranges around
them are not.

0. The one rule this sheet exists to enforce
Stone is ancient and dead. Metal is engineered and alive. Marigold belongs only to what can kill you,
help you, or carry you.
Everything below is a consequence of that sentence. The world splits in two:
Layer	What is in it	Material class	Carries marigold?
Playable layer	track ribbon, boundaries, gaps, obstacle blocks, pickups, projectiles, mines, ships, finish line	hard-surface metal + emissive	Yes — and it is the only layer that does
World layer	monoliths, asteroids, planets, moons, backdrop	stone, concrete, rock	No


The package already states half of this: monoliths and asteroids are "dark stone / worn concrete"
(handoff/02_ENVIRONMENT.md), the track is "dark graphite / black metallic surface"
(handoff/03_TRACK.md). It never states the other half — obstacle blocks, pickups and the finish line
are left on a "shared with track/world" fork that is never taken. This sheet takes it: they are metal.
Why metal and not stone for the hazards: handoff/04_OBSTACLES.md opens by requiring that "obstacle
blocks are immediate gameplay hazards and must read differently from environmental monoliths", then
assigns them a family shared with the world that the monoliths are made of. A material split does that
job for free, at every distance, in every lighting state, with no extra geometry — an engineered thing
sitting on an engineered ribbon, framed by ancient rock that was never built by anyone.
Metal needs something to reflect. Every metal family here assumes an environment source exists in
the scene; a metal surface with nothing to reflect renders black and reads as a hole. This is a
constraint on the lighting rig, not a licence to lower metalness until the problem goes away.
1. Material families
Nine families. Variation comes from roughness, large-scale form, emissive masks and lighting — never
from adding a tenth family (handoff/06_IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES_THREEJS.md: "keep material count low").
Feature scale is given in world units, always. A texture authored to look right on screen is wrong
the moment the camera moves; at 64u of track width and monoliths 200–400u tall, a detail sized by
pixels is invisible on one asset and a rash on the next.
M1 — Track graphite (metal)
The ribbon's floor. The largest surface in frame and the one every other value is judged against.
Property	Target
Class	metal
Metalness	1.0
Roughness	0.35 – 0.50
Base colour	deep graphite, between #0A1117 (deep space) and #303C45 (muted steel)
Panel division scale	8 – 20u — large clean divisions, cut or baked, never a dense grid
Transverse seams	occasional, low contrast, non-emissive
Normal detail	broad only; nothing below ~1u


Restrained gloss is the whole brief: enough reflection to carry warm spill across the dark surface,
never a mirror (handoff/03_TRACK.md). Roughness is the knob — if the floor starts reflecting
recognisable shapes it has gone too low; if the marigold boundary stops bleeding onto the deck it has
gone too high. Interior positioning cues are value and material contrast, not light: low-contrast
panel divisions, restrained roughness variation, and hazard-to-floor contact shading
(handoff/HANDOVER.md §5). No racing line, no safe-route glow, no emissive seam grid.
M2 — Hazard metal (metal)
Standard deadly blocks and destructible blocks. Same family as the track, deliberately a half-step
apart so a block never disappears into the floor it stands on.
Property	Target
Class	metal
Metalness	1.0
Roughness	0.50 – 0.65 — matter than the floor
Base colour	as M1 or a touch darker and cooler
Panel/seam language	sparse, narrow, functional; non-emissive on the standard block
Silhouette	continuous, uninterrupted broad faces


The separation from the floor is carried by roughness, not by colour: a block that is matter than the
deck stays legible when the deck is catching warm reflection, and stays legible when it is not. Do not
buy the separation with a lighter base colour — that costs the "sealed mass" read
(handoff/04_OBSTACLES.md).
Destructible variant. Same family, same 8u envelope, same base values — the difference is
structural, not tonal: a few large sections, broad recessed fractures, and visible interruptions of
the top and side contours. Surface crack texture alone is explicitly insufficient
(handoff/HANDOVER.md §6). Inside the fractures, and only there, M7 appears — recessed, so the energy
is revealed by the break rather than painted on the shell. Fracture interior surfaces are the same
metal with roughness pushed to 0.7 – 0.85: a torn face, not a machined one.
M3 — Dark stone (non-metal)
Monoliths: obelisk, gate, arch. The oldest thing in the frame.
Property	Target
Class	dielectric
Metalness	0.0
Roughness	0.75 – 0.90 — matte to subtle gloss
Base colour	around #303C45 (muted steel) pulled cooler and darker
Panel divisions	large, cut as geometry, 10 – 40u
Wear	near-zero at gameplay distance
Emissive	none — see §3


These are 200–400u tall (handoff/HANDOVER.md §3). At that size, surface texture is nearly free to
omit and nearly impossible to get right: the read is silhouette, occlusion and cold rim light landing on
a broad matte face. "May be almost completely solid if that reads better" (handoff/02_ENVIRONMENT.md)
is not a fallback, it is the target. If a monolith only reads as stone once you are close enough to see
its texture, the form is wrong and no map will save it.
M4 — Worn concrete (non-metal, M3 variant)
The second monolith surface. Built-looking where M3 is carved-looking; use it to keep a monolith field
from reading as one repeated prop.
Property	Target
Class	dielectric
Metalness	0.0
Roughness	0.85 – 0.95
Base colour	M3 lifted slightly and warmed a touch toward neutral grey
Erosion scale	broad, 10 – 40u — soft loss of edge, never fine pitting


Fine pitting and speckle are invisible at monolith distance and become noise at monolith scale. The
variant must be legible as a different surface from 300u away or it is not earning its slot.
M5 — Asteroid rock (non-metal)
Angular, plate, broken; and the elongated, shattered, cluster variations.
Property	Target
Class	dielectric
Metalness	0.0
Roughness	0.80 – 0.95
Base colour	the darkest of the stone families
Fracture faces	same material, roughness at the low end — a fresh break catches more light than a weathered face
Emissive	rare marigold veins only — an accent, not the default state


Size classes run S 10–50u to XL 400u+ (handoff/HANDOVER.md §3). Silhouette beats surface at every one
of them: "avoid noisy micro-detail. The silhouette matters more than the surface at speed"
(handoff/02_ENVIRONMENT.md). The one place surface earns its cost is the contrast between weathered
outer faces and bright fresh fracture planes — that is what says rock rather than dark shape.
The energy-vein variant is the single exception to §3's rule that the world layer carries no marigold.
It is permitted because the package freezes it as a rare accent, and it must stay rare enough that
seeing one is an event. If vein asteroids read as a category rather than an outlier, cut their
frequency, not their brightness.
M6 — Shell (metal)
Pickups, deployed mines, the seeker body, and any small hard-surface object on the playable layer.
Property	Target
Class	metal
Metalness	1.0
Roughness	0.30 – 0.40 — the glossiest family here
Base colour	near-black, cooler than M1
Bevels	crisp, 1 – 3% of the object's smallest dimension, on every silhouette edge
Core	M7, inset and recessed


These objects are small and usually moving, so they have very little screen area in which to prove they
are three-dimensional. Gloss and bevels do that work: a bevel that catches a moving highlight is what
separates a physical object from a flat emissive icon, and an alpha plane or flat icon is explicitly not
an acceptable pickup body (handoff/HANDOVER.md §7). Judge these while rotating, strafing and
approaching — never face-on.
M7 — Marigold emissive
The energy material. One family, three anchors, used across the entire playable layer.
Role	Colour
Primary gainda / marigold	#F59A24
Hot amber	#FFB52E
Bright warm core	#FFE0A0


Structure every emissive element the same way: a #FFE0A0 core, an #FFB52E body, #F59A24 where it
meets the dark shell. That gradient is what makes a small emissive read as hot rather than bright,
and it is why the three anchors exist as a set rather than as alternatives.
Constraints: the perimeter must read golden, never vermilion or red-orange — check it after tone mapping,
not in the source colour (handoff/HANDOVER.md §4). No red hazard code. Cyan is not an alternate energy
family. Bloom is part of the look and is not to be removed to fix haze, but only this family should cross
the bloom threshold — the environment stays below it (handoff/06_IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES_THREEJS.md).
M8 — Gap cavity (non-metal)
The inside of a hole in the floor. Small, and load-bearing far beyond its size.
Property	Target
Class	dielectric
Metalness	0.0
Roughness	1.0 — fully diffuse
Base colour	at or below #0A1117
Reflection	none


A gap is missing geometry, not a dark floor material (handoff/03_TRACK.md). The cavity's entire job is
to fail to catch light: the moment it picks up a highlight or a reflection it reads as a surface you can
fly over, and a 4 × 20u opening becomes a panel seam. Rim treatment is thin marigold edge definition plus
slight inner-lip illumination — and the rim must not be bright enough to perceptually close the opening
(handoff/HANDOVER.md §5).
M9 — Celestial (non-metal)
Gas giants, moons, crescent and eclipse bodies.
Property	Target
Class	dielectric
Metalness	0.0
Roughness	0.90 – 1.0
Base colour	cold and desaturated; no marigold surface colour
Bloom	below threshold, including the lit limb


Compositional anchors, not gameplay objects (handoff/02_ENVIRONMENT.md). They are lit by rim light,
backlight or eclipse, and they must never compete with the playable layer for contrast. A planet bright
enough to bloom has become the subject of the frame, and the subject of the frame is the ship.

3. Emissive policy — marigold is a gameplay signal
Decision (2026-09-19): marigold appears only on the playable layer. Monoliths carry no emissive
seams. The sole exception in the world layer is the rare asteroid energy vein, which the package freezes
as an outlier and which stays one.
The reasoning is a readability contract, not a taste preference. The player is asked to make one
judgement continuously and at speed: is that thing on the track going to kill me, or is it scenery
behind the track? If marigold appears on both sides of that question, the answer has to come from size
and distance cues alone — and the sector concepts deliberately frame monoliths close to the corridor
(handoff/02_ENVIRONMENT.md: monoliths "frame the track", and Monolith Field is a sector in its own
right). Hue is the cheapest, most robust separator available, and it is currently being spent on both
sides of the line.
What this buys elsewhere: with marigold reserved, every emissive mark in frame is information. The track
edge means boundary. A seam in a block means this one breaks. A core means pick this up. A streak
means incoming. That is a vocabulary, and it only works if nothing decorative speaks the same language.
What it costs: monoliths lose a dramatic device at distance. They are 200–400u of dark matte form
against a nebula and must carry their drama through silhouette, scale and cold rim light — which is what
the mood anchor board and "cold rim light, deep shadows" (CURRENT_STATUS.md) ask of them anyway.
Bloom follows the same line: only M7 crosses the threshold. The environment stays below it, and no
material outside the playable layer is permitted to halo.
4. What makes a material correct
A family is finished when all of these hold — judged in the real chase camera, at race speed, with
bloom on and again with bloom off:
1. Stone never reads as metal and metal never reads as stone, at any distance, with no texture
   detail visible.
2. A block is distinguishable from the floor it stands on without relying on its marigold seams, and
   from a monolith without relying on its size.
3. Sealed versus fractured is legible without labels, at A, B and C intensity, with bloom in both
   states.
4. A gap cavity catches no light and never reads as a surface at low camera heights.
5. A pickup reads as a solid object while rotating — bevels catch a moving highlight, side faces are
   shaded differently from front faces.
6. The marigold reads golden after tone mapping, with no reddish spill at the edges.
7. No surface outside the playable layer blooms.
Roughness, base colour and bevel values are tuned against these reads and then frozen back into §1.
A value that passes a static screenshot and fails at speed has failed.
5. Deferred, deliberately
- Ship hulls. "Ships and dedicated orthographic passes remain a separate art track"
  (handoff/HANDOVER.md §1). This sheet pre-assigns them the M6 family with M7 engines so the
  ship track inherits a surface language rather than inventing a tenth material — the designs themselves
  stay open, and per-class proportions differ sharply (Comet is wider than long).
- HUD and UI. Graphite and marigold is the whole of the current direction; typography, layout,
  motion and the rear-view treatment are open (CURRENT_STATUS.md).
- Numeric fidelity budgets. No mesh, draw-call or frame-time budget is invented here, matching the
  package's own refusal to invent one.
6. Overrides — what this sheet changes, and why
Three, all narrow. Everything else in docs/art-direction/ stands.
1. Obstacle blocks are metal (M2), not "dark material family shared with track/world."
   handoff/04_OBSTACLES.md leaves the family ambiguous between the two material classes it names. That
   ambiguity sits directly on top of the requirement, in the same document, that blocks "must read
   differently from environmental monoliths". Resolved toward metal.
2. Monoliths carry no marigold seams. This overrides handoff/02_ENVIRONMENT.md's "sparse marigold
   seams only" and handoff/HANDOVER.md §4's "include selective glowing seams". Rationale in §3: the
   package assigns "sparse marigold seams" to standard deadly blocks and to monoliths, which spends
   the strongest available separator on both sides of the one distinction the obstacle brief exists to
   enforce.
3. Pickups, weapon shells and the finish line are metal (M6 / M1–M2). The package describes a "dark
   structural shell" with "beveled/shaded sides" and asks the finish line to use "the same track/world
   language", without naming a family for either. Named here.
No override touches a silhouette, a frozen form family, a dimension, a colour anchor, or the A/B/C
intensity language.
